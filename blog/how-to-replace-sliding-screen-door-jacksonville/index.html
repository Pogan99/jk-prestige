How to Replace a Sliding Screen Door in Jacksonville

August 18, 2026 · 6 min read · JK Prestige Constructor Corp

If your sliding patio screen door keeps skipping off its track, sagging at the corners, or letting in every no-see-um from the yard, you are not alone. Between salt air near the beaches, sandy grit off yards near the marsh, and Jacksonville's year-round UV load, screen doors here wear out fast. The good news is that a sliding screen door is one of the more forgiving DIY repairs on a house. Most homeowners can pull the old door, clean up the frame and track, and rehang a new or rescreened door in an afternoon with basic tools. This guide covers measuring the frame, adjusting the rollers, cleaning the track, and picking the right mesh for a Northeast Florida yard.

When You Need a Permit in Jacksonville

Swapping a sliding screen door for a new one, or rescreening the panel you already have, is routine maintenance in Duval County. You are not altering the structure or the opening, so no permit is required.

What does trigger a permit is different work sometimes confused with a screen door swap. Replacing the actual glass sliding patio door is a change to a structural opening and needs a permit. Building or modifying a screen enclosure or pool cage is also structural work, permitted separately, since it involves posts, footers, and roof or wall attachment. If your patio sits next to a pool, note that a sliding screen door is not a recognized barrier under Florida's pool safety code and does not count toward pool enclosure requirements, even if it is the only thing between the pool deck and the house.

If you are ever unsure whether your project crosses from maintenance into permitted work, the City of Jacksonville Building Inspection Division is the right place to confirm before you start.

Tools and Materials

  • Tape measure
  • Flathead and Phillips screwdrivers
  • Spline roller tool and utility knife
  • Replacement screen mesh (fiberglass, pet-resistant, or no-see-um)
  • Vinyl spline sized to your door's channel
  • Shop vacuum or stiff brush
  • Silicone-based track lubricant
  • Replacement roller wheels, if worn or cracked

Step-by-Step

  1. Measure the opening. Measure the height and width of the existing frame, not the rough opening, at three points each way, since older frames can be slightly out of square. Note the roller style, since hardware varies by builder.
  1. Remove the old door. Lift the door, angle the bottom out of the lower track, then swing it free of the top track.
  1. Clean and inspect the frame. Check the corners for cracked welds or bent aluminum, common after a door gets caught by wind. If sound, reuse it and just replace the mesh and spline.
  1. Pull the old spline and mesh. Pry up an end of the spline with the flathead screwdriver and pull it from the channel. The old mesh lifts out once the spline is gone.
  1. Lay in the new mesh and spline. Center the mesh over the frame with a couple inches of overhang, press it into the channel with the convex wheel of the spline roller, then roll the spline in on top with the concave wheel. Keep the mesh taut but not distorted.
  1. Trim the excess mesh. Run a utility knife along the outside edge of the spline channel, angled away from the frame, for a clean cut.
  1. Adjust or replace the rollers. Most doors have adjustment screws near the bottom corners that raise or lower the door in the track. Replace a cracked or flattened wheel rather than fighting the adjustment.
  1. Clean the track. Vacuum out sand, dead insects, and debris, then wipe with a damp cloth. Skip oil-based lubricants, which grab grit.
  1. Rehang the door. Angle the top into the upper track first, set the rollers into the bottom track, and let it drop into place. Slide it to confirm it rolls smoothly and latches without binding.

Jacksonville Climate Considerations

Homes near the beaches, the Intracoastal, and the St. Johns River see salt air that corrodes aluminum frames and roller hardware faster than inland, so rinse the frame and track periodically near water. Year-round UV makes vinyl spline and standard fiberglass mesh brittle and crack years before the frame fails, usually the real reason a screen starts shedding at the corners. Sand and pollen, especially in spring, pack into the track and roller housing, often the actual cause of a door that feels "stuck" rather than broken. Near the marshes and river, no-see-ums and mosquitoes are a real nuisance, and standard mesh has gaps wide enough for them to pass through. A screen door left open during a storm or a squall off the river can also get torn off its track, so close it before weather rolls in.

Most Jacksonville homeowners do better with a heavier-duty mesh than standard fiberglass. Pet-resistant mesh is a vinyl-coated polyester weave that resists scratching from dogs and cats. No-see-um mesh uses a tighter weave to keep out biting midges, though it reduces airflow slightly. Some homeowners use pet-resistant mesh on the main door and reserve no-see-um mesh for porches or lanais where bugs are the bigger issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to replace a sliding screen door?
For an experienced DIYer with a frame in good shape, rescreening usually takes 30 to 60 minutes. A full door and hardware swap, including adjusting rollers and cleaning the track, typically takes one to two hours. A contractor doing the same work usually finishes in under an hour per door.
Can I just replace the mesh instead of the whole door?
Yes, and it's cheaper and faster, as long as the frame is straight, the corners are solid, and the rollers still work. If the frame is bent, corroded, or the track is worn out, replacing the mesh alone will not fix the underlying problem.
What mesh holds up best against Jacksonville bugs and pets?
A vinyl-coated pet-resistant mesh holds up far better than standard fiberglass screen against scratching and everyday wear. If biting no-see-ums and small mosquitoes near the marsh or river are the bigger concern, a tighter no-see-um weave keeps them out more effectively, though it cuts down on airflow a little compared to standard mesh.
